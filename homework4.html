1. Stochastic Penetration Process Formulation

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space. Consider $n$ systems (or consecutive security tiers / intrusion attempts) probed by $m$ independent attackers. For each attacker $j \in \{1, \dots, m\}$, the penetration process is modeled as a discrete-time stochastic process $\{S_k^{(j)}\}_{k=0}^n$ with $S_0^{(j)} = 0$ and:

$$S_k^{(j)} = \sum_{i=1}^k X_i^{(j)}, \quad k = 1, 2, \dots, n$$

where $\{X_i^{(j)}\}_{i=1}^n$ is a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables representing the penetration increment at step $i$:

  • Bernoulli Model: $X_i \in \{0, 1\}$ with $P(X_i = 1) = p$ and $P(X_i = 0) = 1 - p$. Here, $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = p$ and $\text{Var}(X_i) = p(1-p)$. The score represents a unilateral firewall traversal where an attacker either gains $+1$ access or is blocked with $0$ incremental progress.
  • Rademacher Model: $X_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ with $P(X_i = +1) = P(X_i = -1) = 1/2$. Here, $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = 0$ and $\text{Var}(X_i) = 1$. This models a symmetric zero-sum duel: an attacker either breaches an asset ($+1$) or the automated defense detects and expels the intrusion ($-1$).

2. The Law of Large Numbers (LLN) and Convergence in Probability

Consider the sample average penetration score $\bar{X}_n = \frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. By linearity of expectation and independence:

$$\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{S_n}{n}\right] = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i] = \mu, \qquad \text{Var}\left(\frac{S_n}{n}\right) = \frac{1}{n^2} \sum_{i=1}^n \text{Var}(X_i) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$$

Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN): For any arbitrary tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$, the probability that the average score deviates from the true mean $\mu$ by more than $\varepsilon$ vanishes as the number of systems $n \to \infty$:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} P\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n} - \mu\right| \ge \varepsilon\right) = 0$$

Proof via Chebyshev's Inequality: For any random variable $Y$ with mean $\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mu$ and finite variance $\text{Var}(Y)$, Chebyshev's inequality guarantees $P(|Y - \mu| \ge \varepsilon) \le \frac{\text{Var}(Y)}{\varepsilon^2}$. Applying this directly to $\bar{X}_n$:

$$P\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n} - \mu\right| \ge \varepsilon\right) \le \frac{\text{Var}(S_n / n)}{\varepsilon^2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{n \varepsilon^2} \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0$$

In our interactive simulation, this is directly confirmed: as $n$ grows from 25 to 2000, all simulated attacker paths condense into an increasingly narrow "funnel" centered at $\mu$, and the empirical proportion outside $\pm \varepsilon$ consistently respects Chebyshev's theoretical upper bound.

3. Behavior of the Variance Under the Three Normalizations

A central insight of probability theory is understanding how scaling factors $c_n$ affect the dispersion of aggregate sums. Let $Y_n = c_n S_n$. The variance transforms as:

$$\text{Var}(Y_n) = \text{Var}(c_n S_n) = c_n^2 \text{Var}(S_n) = c_n^2 \cdot n \sigma^2$$

We observe three distinct asymptotic regimes:

  • 1. Unscaled Total Score ($c_n = 1$): $\text{Var}(S_n) = n \sigma^2$. The variance grows linearly with $n$ ($\text{Var} \to \infty$). Dispersion widens without bound, producing a spreading cloud of trajectories with standard deviation $\sigma \sqrt{n}$.
  • 2. Average Score ($c_n = 1/n$): $\text{Var}(S_n/n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$. The variance collapses hyperbolically to zero ($\text{Var} \to 0$). All sample trajectories converge almost surely to the deterministic constant $\mu$ (Law of Large Numbers).
  • 3. Diffusive Normalized Score ($c_n = 1/\sqrt{n}$): $\text{Var}(S_n/\sqrt{n}) = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^2 \cdot (n \sigma^2) = \sigma^2$. The variance is exactly scale-invariant across all $n$! In the Rademacher model where $\sigma^2 = 1$, $\text{Var}(S_n/\sqrt{n}) \equiv 1$ for every $n$.

Thus, $\sqrt{n}$ is the unique critical scaling exponent: dividing by any power smaller than $1/2$ causes variance to diverge, while dividing by any power larger than $1/2$ causes variance to degenerate to zero.

4. Central Limit Theorem (CLT) & Standard Normal Convergence

The Lindeberg-Lévy Central Limit Theorem asserts that for any sequence of i.i.d. random variables with mean $\mu$ and finite variance $\sigma^2 > 0$, the standardized sum converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$:

$$Z_n = \frac{S_n - n\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

For the Rademacher model, $\mu = 0$ and $\sigma = 1$. The normalized sum is already centered and standardized:

$$Z_n = \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$$

Proof Sketch via Characteristic Functions: The characteristic function of a single Rademacher increment is $\varphi_X(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{itX}] = \frac{e^{it} + e^{-it}}{2} = \cos(t)$. The characteristic function of $Z_n = S_n/\sqrt{n}$ is:

$$\varphi_{Z_n}(t) = \left[\varphi_X\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]^n = \left[\cos\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]^n$$

Using Taylor series expansion $\cos(u) = 1 - \frac{u^2}{2} + O(u^4)$:

$$\varphi_{Z_n}(t) = \left[1 - \frac{t^2}{2n} + O\left(\frac{t^4}{n^2}\right)\right]^n \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} e^{-t^2/2}$$

Since $e^{-t^2/2}$ is uniquely the characteristic function of the standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, Lévy's Continuity Theorem establishes convergence in distribution: $Z_n \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.

Speed of Convergence (Berry-Esseen Theorem): For the Rademacher model, the third central moment $\mathbb{E}[|X|^3] = 1$, and skewness is identically zero due to symmetry. The Berry-Esseen theorem bounds the uniform deviation between the empirical CDF $F_n(x)$ and the standard normal CDF $\Phi(x)$:

$$\sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} |F_n(x) - \Phi(x)| \le \frac{C \cdot \mathbb{E}[|X - \mu|^3]}{\sigma^3 \sqrt{n}} = O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)$$

This rapid $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ convergence is evidenced on our Q-Q Plot and Kolmogorov-Smirnov test: already at $n = 50$, the normalized Rademacher sum matches the Gaussian curve with high fidelity.

5. Cybersecurity Interpretation: System Scale $n$ and Adversary Fleet $m$

LLN Convergence $\bar{X}_n \xrightarrow{P} p \quad [O(1/n)]$

1. Predictable Per-Layer Breach Density

As defense depth $n$ expands, per-layer attacker penetration deterministically condenses toward the baseline probability $p$.

Mathematical Mechanism & Limit

By Kolmogorov's Strong Law of Large Numbers, the empirical average score $\bar{X}_n = \frac{S_n}{n}$ converges almost surely to the Bernoulli expectation: $\lim_{n \to \infty} P(|\bar{X}_n - p| \ge \varepsilon) = 0$. Variance collapses hyperbolically at rate $\text{Var}(\bar{X}_n) = \frac{p(1-p)}{n} = O(1/n) \to 0$.

Enterprise Architecture Scenario

In a zero-trust multi-tier architecture with $n = 500$ inspection layers (API gateways, mTLS handshakes, host firewalls), individual attacker trajectories lose random volatility. The fraction of traversed defenses stabilizes tightly around $p$, eliminating erratic anomaly spikes across the macro enterprise.

Tactical SOC Countermeasure

Security Operations Centers can reliably budget triage capacity and SIEM log ingestion rates: average incident flow is strictly bounded by $n \cdot p$. Chebyshev's bound guarantees that anomalous surges beyond $p \pm \varepsilon$ occur with frequency $\le \frac{\sigma^2}{n \varepsilon^2}$.

Exposure Scaling $\mathbb{E}[S_n] = np \quad [O(n)]$

2. Total Enterprise Exposure Explosion

Unscaled breach count grows linearly $\mathbb{E}[S_n] = np$. Expanding cloud footprint without lowering $p$ creates an unmanageable incident avalanche.

Mathematical Mechanism & Limit

The unnormalized sum $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ accumulates raw variance $\text{Var}(S_n) = n \sigma^2 = n p (1-p)$. Standard deviation spreads as $\sigma \sqrt{n}$. Because variance diverges linearly ($O(n) \to \infty$), individual adversary penetration depths diverge without bound across an expanding attack surface.

Enterprise Architecture Scenario

An enterprise migrating from 100 on-prem servers to 2,000 Kubernetes microservices ($n = 2000$) multiplies total security incident count 20-fold if per-hop breach probability $p = 0.08$ remains constant: expected breaches rise from $8$ to $160$ per adversary campaign.

Tactical SOC Countermeasure

Infrastructure growth must be balanced by an aggressive sub-linear breach reduction: hardening policies must depress $p$ faster than $1/n$ (e.g. strict least-privilege RBAC, automated credential rotation). If $p(n) = O(1/n)$, expected total exposure $\mathbb{E}[S_n] = n \cdot p(n) = O(1)$ remains bounded regardless of fleet scale.

Donsker Invariance $S_n/\sqrt{n} \Rightarrow W_t \quad [O(\sqrt{n})]$

3. Adversarial Parity & Brownian Lateral Excursions

Under zero-sum active defense ($P(+1) = P(-1) = 1/2$), net drift is zero, yet natural Brownian excursions swing by $\pm \sqrt{n}$ tiers, opening lateral compromise windows.

Mathematical Mechanism & Limit

In the Rademacher model, increments have zero mean ($\mu = 0$) and unit variance ($\sigma^2 = 1$). Donsker's Invariance Principle (Functional CLT) establishes that the continuous-time process $B_n(t) = \frac{S_{\lfloor nt \rfloor}}{\sqrt{n}}$ converges weakly to standard Brownian motion $W(t)$. The scale-invariant normalization $\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ preserves variance identically at $1.00$.

Enterprise Architecture Scenario

Even when automated endpoint detection and response (EDR) blocks half of all exploits ($P = 0.5$), an enterprise with $n = 2500$ internal micro-segments experiences natural random fluctuations reaching $\pm 2\sqrt{2500} = \pm 100$ tiers. An adversary can ride this transient excursion into core data vaults before defensive measures neutralize them.

Tactical SOC Countermeasure

Do not rely on net-zero equilibrium. Deploy automated lateral movement velocity tripwires: if an asset's traversal increment rate exceeds the expected diffusion envelope $+2\sqrt{k}$, trigger instantaneous micro-quarantine of the session token, truncating the random walk before peak excursion.

Extreme Value Tail $1 - (1-p_T)^m \to 100\%$

4. The Adversary Fleet Multiplier & Tail Collapse

Cybersecurity is an extreme-value problem: defense must stop all $m$ attackers, while an adversary needs only a single trajectory exceeding threshold $T$.

Mathematical Mechanism & Limit

Let single-attacker tail breach probability be $p_T = P(S_n \ge T) \approx 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{T - n\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}}\right)$. For $m$ independent adversaries, the maximum penetration $M_m = \max_{j=1\dots m} S_n^{(j)}$ governs security. The probability of enterprise breach is $P(M_m \ge T) = 1 - (1 - p_T)^m$. As fleet size $m \to \infty$, $P(\text{Breach}) \to 1$ exponentially fast.

Enterprise Architecture Scenario

Suppose a perimeter wall sets threshold $T$ such that single-attacker breach odds are merely $0.5\%$ ($p_T = 0.005$). Against a coordinated botnet or distributed denial-of-service swarm of $m = 600$ attacker agents, enterprise breach probability becomes $1 - (1 - 0.005)^{600} = 1 - 0.049 = 95.1\%$. The defense fails with near certainty!

Tactical SOC Countermeasure

Because raising the threshold $T$ only increases protection linearly or square-root, while adversary volume $m$ drives failure exponentially, edge ingress defenses must focus on fleet deflation: automated IP reputation blocking, proof-of-work CAPTCHAs, and API rate-limiting to depress $m$ before adversaries touch interior systems.
