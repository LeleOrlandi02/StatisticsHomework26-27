Welford’s Recurrence Formulas for Mean, Sum of Squares & Variance

Deriving the single-pass update rules that prevent catastrophic floating-point cancellation.

1. Derivation of Knuth's Recurrence Formula for the Mean

Let $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_k$ denote a stream of numerical observations. The classical sample mean after $k$ observations is:

Equation 1: Sample Mean Definition
$$\bar{x}_k = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k x_i$$

To compute $\bar{x}_k$ sequentially without storing the history of previous values, we isolate the first $k-1$ terms from the new incoming data point $x_k$:

Algebraic Steps: Mean Recurrence
$$\begin{aligned} \bar{x}_k &= \frac{1}{k} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} x_i + x_k \right) \\ &= \frac{1}{k} \left( (k-1)\bar{x}_{k-1} + x_k \right) \\ &= \frac{k-1}{k}\bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{x_k}{k} \\ &= \left( 1 - \frac{1}{k} \right)\bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{x_k}{k} \\ &= \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k} \end{aligned}$$

This is Knuth's update rule for the mean. The correction step adds the scaled prediction error $\frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}$ to the previous estimate $\bar{x}_{k-1}$.

2. Derivation of Welford's Recurrence Formula for the Sum of Squares ($M_{2,k} \equiv SS_k$)

The sum of squared deviations from the running mean after $k$ observations is defined as:

Equation 2: Sum of Squared Deviations (SS)
$$M_{2,k} \equiv SS_k = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2$$

Separating the first $k-1$ elements from the $k$-th observation:

$$M_{2,k} = \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2 + (x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2$$

Notice that the deviation from the new mean can be decomposed using the previous mean $\bar{x}_{k-1}$:

$$(x_i - \bar{x}_k) = (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) - (\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})$$

Squaring this expression and summing over $i = 1, \dots, k-1$:

Algebraic Steps: Expanding the Partial Sum
$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2 &= \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} \left[ (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) - (\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}) \right]^2 \\ &= \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 - 2(\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}) \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1})}_{= 0} + (k-1)(\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 \\ &= M_{2,k-1} + (k-1)(\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 \end{aligned}$$

The cross-term vanishes identically because $\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) \equiv 0$ by definition of the sample mean! Now, substituting this back into $M_{2,k}$ alongside the $k$-th term $(x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2$:

$$M_{2,k} = M_{2,k-1} + (k-1)(\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 + (x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2$$

From Knuth's update rule, we have $\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1} = \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}$. Furthermore:

$$x_k - \bar{x}_k = x_k - \left( \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k} \right) = (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})\left(1 - \frac{1}{k}\right) = \frac{k-1}{k}(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})$$

Substituting both relations into our remaining two terms:

Algebraic Steps: Combining Remaining Terms
$$\begin{aligned} (k-1)(\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 + (x_k - \bar{x}_k)^2 &= (k-1)\left(\frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{k-1}{k}(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})\right)^2 \\ &= (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 \left[ \frac{k-1}{k^2} + \frac{(k-1)^2}{k^2} \right] \\ &= (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 \left[ \frac{(k-1)(1 + k - 1)}{k^2} \right] \\ &= (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 \left[ \frac{(k-1)k}{k^2} \right] \\ &= \frac{k-1}{k} (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 \end{aligned}$$

Finally, observing that $\frac{k-1}{k}(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}) = (x_k - \bar{x}_k)$, we obtain the celebrated product form:

Equation 3: Welford's Sum of Squares Recurrence (1962)
$$M_{2,k} = M_{2,k-1} + (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}) \cdot (x_k - \bar{x}_k)$$

3. Recurrence for Sample Variance $s_k^2$ & Bessel's Correction

The unbiased sample variance with Bessel's correction $k-1$ is obtained immediately from $M_{2,k}$:

Equation 4: Sample Variance & Direct Recurrence
$$s_k^2 = \frac{M_{2,k}}{k - 1} \quad (\text{for } k \ge 2)$$ $$s_k^2 = \frac{k-2}{k-1}s_{k-1}^2 + \frac{(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2}{k}$$

In contrast, the biased population variance estimator divides by $k$: $\sigma_k^2 = \frac{M_{2,k}}{k} = \frac{k-1}{k}s_k^2$.

The 3-Line Core Streaming Algorithm

As requested, the core mathematical update requires only about 3 lines of code inside the observation stream loop:

JavaScript / C / Python Core Loop O(1) Time • O(1) Memory 
// Inside a stream receiving incoming observations x with counter k:
const delta  = x - mean;
mean       += delta / k;
M2         += delta * (x - mean);

// Sample Variance for k >= 2 is: M2 / (k - 1)

1. Conclusions from Comparing Average Estimates with Theoretical $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$

Across our $m$ independent simulated hackers, the average of the sampling means $\overline{\bar{x}} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m \bar{x}^{(j)}$ matches the theoretical distribution mean $\mu$ with remarkable precision (relative error typically $< 0.1\%$). Likewise, the average of the sampling variances $\overline{s^2} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m s^{2(j)}$ closely tracks the theoretical variance $\sigma^2$.

This confirms two cornerstone theorems of mathematical statistics:

Equation 5: Unbiasedness of Sample Mean & Sample Variance
$$\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}] = \mu, \qquad \mathbb{E}[S^2] = \sigma^2$$

The Role of Bessel's Correction: If we calculate variance using the naive denominator $n$ instead of $n-1$, the expected value evaluates to:

$$\mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})^2 \right] = \frac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2 = \sigma^2 - \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$$

Because the sample mean $\bar{X}$ is calculated from the exact same sample observations, it automatically minimizes the sum of squared deviations $\sum (X_i - c)^2$ around $c = \bar{X}$ rather than around the true population mean $\mu$. This introduces a systematic downward bias of $-\frac{\sigma^2}{n}$. Welford’s recurrence incorporating Bessel's correction $\frac{M_2}{n-1}$ perfectly compensates for this loss of one degree of freedom, rendering $S^2$ exactly unbiased for all sample sizes $n \ge 2$.

2. Why These Results Are Fundamental in Inferential Statistics

In practical cybersecurity telemetry, machine learning, and empirical research, the true parameters $(\mu, \sigma^2)$ of the environment are unknown. We must infer them from finite sample observations $x_1, \dots, x_n$.

  • Guaranteed Absence of Systematic Distortion: Unbiasedness ensures that an estimation procedure will not consistently over- or under-estimate true adversary strength or system breach vulnerability over repeated deployments.
  • Construction of Valid Confidence Intervals: When population variance $\sigma^2$ is unknown (the standard real-world scenario), replacing $\sigma$ with the sample standard deviation $S$ yields the pivotal statistic:
    $$T = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{S / \sqrt{n}} \sim t_{n-1}$$
    which follows Student's $t$-distribution with $n-1$ degrees of freedom. Without Welford's exact unbiased computation, confidence intervals $\bar{X} \pm t_{\alpha/2, n-1}\frac{S}{\sqrt{n}}$ would suffer from distorted coverage probabilities!
  • Hypothesis Testing & ANOVA: Analysis of Variance ($F$-tests) and two-sample $t$-tests rely fundamentally on comparing independent sample variances. Accurate variance estimation is prerequisite to detecting whether a new security patch significantly reduces exploit scores.
  • Streaming Cybersecurity Telemetry: In automated Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems monitoring millions of network packets per second, buffering all packets to compute two-pass statistics is impossible. Welford's algorithm provides exact, drift-free running baselines with $O(1)$ memory and $O(1)$ computation per packet.

3. How System Size $n$ Affects the Variability of Sampling Means & Variances

As each hacker attempts an increasing number of systems $n$, the variability of both sampling estimators contracts dramatically according to specific power-law decay rates:

Equation 6: Asymptotic Variance Scaling Laws
$$\text{Var}(\bar{X}_n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} = O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right), \qquad \text{SE}(\bar{X}_n) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)$$ $$\text{Var}(S_n^2) = \frac{1}{n}\left( \mu_4 - \frac{n-3}{n-1}\sigma^4 \right) \approx \frac{2\sigma^4}{n-1} = O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)$$

Observations from the Interactive Simulation:

  • Concentration of Sample Means ($n \to \infty$): For small $n = 10$, individual hacker means fluctuate widely across $[2, 8]$. By the time $n = 500$ or $1000$, all hacker trajectories condense into an extremely narrow band tightly hugging $\mu$. The standard error $\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$ shrinks by $\sqrt{100} = 10\times$ as $n$ scales from $10$ to $1000$.
  • The Law of Diminishing Returns: Because standard error contracts as $1/\sqrt{n}$, halving the uncertainty in estimating adversary skill requires four times as many target systems ($4n$).
  • Consistency of Sample Variance: The histogram of sample variances $\{s_n^{2(j)}\}_{j=1}^m$ sharpens from a broad, dispersed distribution at $n = 10$ into a needle-like spike at $n = 1000$, proving that $S_n^2 \xrightarrow{P} \sigma^2$ (the estimator is statistically consistent).
